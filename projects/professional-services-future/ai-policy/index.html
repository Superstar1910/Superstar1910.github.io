AI Governance & Acceptable Use Policy

A practical policy template for professional services firms — law, accounting, audit, consulting and investment advisory.

Adoption notice: This is a general template. Replace placeholders, conduct jurisdiction-specific legal review, obtain board or partnership approval, and operationalise controls before adoption. It is not legal advice or a certification of compliance.

1. Purpose and scope

The firm supports responsible AI to improve quality, efficiency and client outcomes. This policy covers partners, employees and contractors using generative AI, machine learning, predictive systems, embedded AI or agents for firm work. AI does not displace professional accountability.

2. Governance principles

Professional accountability, confidentiality, privacy, accuracy, transparency where required, human oversight, security, fairness and proportionate controls.

3. Risk classification and approvals

ClassExamplesMinimum control
LowNon-confidential brainstorming, formattingApproved tool; trained user
ModerateInternal research, routine first draftsManager oversight; source checks
HighClient advice, valuations, regulated analysis, privileged/sensitive informationEngagement partner and Risk/Compliance approval; documented review
ProhibitedUnauthorised disclosure, fabricated evidence, unapproved consequential or autonomous decisionsDo not deploy

Assess both task and data sensitivity.

4. Acceptable use

Use approved tools only for authorised work. Never submit confidential, personal or privileged information to unapproved services; fabricate authorities or evidence; submit unchecked client deliverables; or allow agents to execute binding actions beyond their authority.

5. Confidentiality, privacy and IP

Apply data minimisation, access restrictions, contractual protections, retention and deletion controls, transfer assessments and vendor model-training restrictions. Approval of a tool does not authorise all information categories.

6. Human review and quality assurance

Qualified reviewers verify authoritative sources, legal citations, factual claims, financial calculations, assumptions, completeness and jurisdiction. Document approval for high-risk outputs.

7. AI agents and automated workflows

Assign accountable owners, permissions, audit logs, approval gates, escalation procedures and shutdown mechanisms. External communications, filings, transfers, contract execution and system changes require explicit authorisation.

8. Vendor approval

Assess security, processing terms, data location, training rights, reliability, auditability, resilience, costs and exit arrangements. Maintain an approved-systems register.

9. Staff competency and sensitisation

Provide induction, role-specific training, hands-on verification exercises and refreshers. Train partners and managers to supervise AI-assisted work, not merely operate AI tools.

10. Accountability

Board/partnership sets risk appetite; CEO/managing partner implements; AI lead manages registers; Risk/Legal/Compliance verifies duties; IT/Security oversees systems; engagement partners supervise professional work; users report incidents.

11. Incidents and escalation

Report suspected disclosures, fabricated outputs, unauthorised actions or security incidents. Contain, preserve evidence, assess impact, notify when required and document remediation.

12. Monitoring and enforcement

Track quality, rework, time, review effort, technology costs, realised benefits, incidents and competencies. Hours saved do not automatically become profit. Review policy at least annually. Violations may result in suspended access, training or discipline.

Need help putting this policy into practice?

A written policy is the starting point. Firms also need approved workflows, governance ownership and staff who understand what they can and cannot do with AI.
