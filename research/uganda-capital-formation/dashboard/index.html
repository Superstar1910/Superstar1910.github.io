Our purpose

Turn national ambition into investable assets.

We want Uganda’s US$500bn by 2040 ambition to become a practical capital formation programme: credible investment requirements, well-prepared projects, suitable financing and measurable results.

01 / ESTABLISH THE EVIDENCE

A shared investment framework

Test growth and investment assumptions, distinguish existing flows from additional needs, and reconcile financing sources without double counting.

02 / ENABLE TRANSACTIONS

More viable projects financed

Connect project preparation, risk allocation and investor requirements so suitable assets can reach financial close and operation.

03 / SUSTAIN EXECUTION

Institutional delivery mandates

Build a sustainable role for Oakstone through scoped diagnostics, project preparation, vehicle structuring and programme implementation.

Current stage: research and engagement. We are seeking informed feedback and institutional pilot discussions. No pilot mandate, partner appointment or financing commitment is claimed.

Longer-term objective

A repeatable system that prepares assets, mobilises appropriate capital and tracks operating results: productivity, exports, jobs and investor returns.

Success means better evidence, institutions engaged, pilots commissioned, projects prepared and transactions completed. Capital committed, capital disbursed and new productive investment will be reported separately. Economic outcomes follow operating assets, not fundraising announcements.

The investment challenge

Capital becomes growth when it becomes productive.

01 / MEASURE

Investment is an annual flow

Gross capital formation covers fixed assets and inventory changes. It differs from the stock of savings and financial assets.

02 / PREPARE

Build assets investors can finance

Demand, land, permits, contracts, credible models and risk allocation turn an opportunity into a financeable project.

03 / DELIVER

Measure what investment produces

Track operating capacity, exports, jobs, productivity and repayments alongside financial close and disbursements.

How the model works — and what it cannot establish

Required nominal dollar CAGR = (target / starting GDP)^(1 / years) − 1. Annual GDP compounds at that rate. Annual investment = GDP × investment share. The comparison holds the starting investment share constant; it is conditional on the same GDP path and is not secured financing.

The article preset instead uses piecewise linear GDP and investment shares across its checkpoints; its CAGR is only an endpoint-implied calculation. Cumulative investment sums annual flows after the starting year through 2040. The difference is additional investment relative to the comparison scenario, not a measured financing gap or foreign capital requirement. A negative difference means the scenario invests less.

Investment does not mechanically cause this GDP path. Real growth, domestic GDP inflation and currency movements must be modelled separately. Approximate dollar growth factor = (1 + real growth) × (1 + GDP inflation) / (1 + currency depreciation).

Proposed roadmap · Owners to be agreed

Scoped 90 / 100-day design

  1. Validate historical GDP, capital formation and financing data; publish reproducible calculations.
  2. Map existing institutions and facilities before proposing new mechanisms.
  3. Screen an initial pipeline using common readiness criteria.
  4. Run the national 90-day sprint and linked retirement 100-day diagnostic.
  5. Publish the first scorecard and assign accountable delivery owners.

Measure delivery

Funding committed and disbursed; financial closes; time to close; operating assets; exports and jobs; repayments and fiscal risk.
