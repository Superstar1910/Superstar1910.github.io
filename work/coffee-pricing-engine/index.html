RESEARCH PROGRAMME / FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE

Kenneth S. Legesi, CFA · Updated 30 October 2026 · Research and working prototype

Coffee Pricing Engine: From Yield per Tree to Buyer Quote

A research-led financial intelligence platform designed to help coffee farmers, processors, aggregators, traders, exporters and buyers understand the economics of coffee production and trade. Its ambition is to connect the physical movement of coffee with the financial costs, risks and prices borne by each participant.

Know Your Cost Before You Quote. Try the public research calculator. Its outputs are illustrative and should not be treated as independently verified market prices or executable quotations.

Why this project exists

Price information alone does not reveal profitability. The price of fresh cherry, dried Kiboko, parchment, unsorted FAQ and graded green coffee refers to different physical products and weights. Milling recovery, moisture, grade mix, supplier payments, storage, financing, FX and delivery terms change the effective cost of an accepted saleable kilogram. A futures reference is not a farmgate price, and a buyer's bid is not the seller's netback.

Who the engine serves

  • Farmers and estates: productive trees, acreage, harvests and cash, accounting and economic cost per green-equivalent kilogram; yield and farm-level break-even.
  • Processors and hullers: measured input weight, moisture removal, rejected material, grade recovery, batch reconciliation and grade-specific cost allocation.
  • Aggregators and traders: lot acquisition costs, logistics, inventory holding, dated working capital, minimum quotes and physical buyer netbacks.
  • Exporters: contract specifications, destination, compliance, carriage, insurance, FX and commercial margin under agreed delivery terms.
  • Buyers and roasters: comparable offers, supplier quality and landed acquisition costs, with roast economics reserved for later development.

How the engine is designed

  1. Farm economics: trees → measured crop production → cash, accounting and opportunity costs.
  2. Physical transformation: cherry, Kiboko or parchment → moisture reconciliation → saleable green output and grade mix.
  3. Commercial transaction: procurement → processing → warehousing → transport → dated finance and contract obligations.
  4. Market intelligence: separate official indicative prices, futures references, observed trades and executable physical buyer offers with source, date, currency, grade and delivery basis.
  5. Decision and governance: full unit cost, break-even, walk-away, target quotation, netback and hold/sell sensitivity with traceable assumptions.

These layers are a target architecture, not a claim that every feature is already deployed. One deterministic costing kernel is intended to serve distinct, role-led user journeys. Source provenance, unit comparability and independent review remain central design requirements.

What works today

The research MVP includes farm-year unit costing, measured batch conversion and multi-grade allocation, wet-basis moisture calculations, manual costing and quotation, alternative dated financing methods, advisory Incoterm checks, sell-versus-hold scenarios, contextual input help and downloadable assumption records. A role selector prioritises relevant research panels; a novice glossary and a labelled manual market-reference panel explain key concepts. The app does not independently authenticate the inputs or source labels.

What is being developed

The next releases seek to connect farm and processing records into one reconciled transaction ledger, fully differentiate buyer/exporter workflows, allocate seller costs by actual contractual delivery obligations, and integrate approved market-price feeds subject to data rights and freshness controls. Authenticated scenarios, independently verified quotes and performance back-testing are longer-term goals. There is currently no validated national Ugandan production-cost distribution, live executable buyer-price feed or production-approved quote process.

Research, methods and terminology

The working paper, The Economics of Green Coffee: From Yield per Tree to Export Price (v0.6), defines the methodological and validation programme. The underlying research also includes a global catalogue of 39 identifiable pricing applications and reference systems, an architecture blueprint, a source register and a beginner's glossary. Some repository material is private; a public summary and the calculator are available here without releasing confidential records.

Published essays: Coffee Is Traded, Not Just Grown and What Does a Kilogram of Coffee Really Cost?. A six-essay research series is prepared for weekly publication from 6 November to 11 December 2026; future-dated essays should not be described as published until they are live.

Evidence and collaboration

We seek anonymised farm-year records showing trees, acreage, harvests and production costs; mill tickets linking input form, measured weights, moisture and grade outturn; dated and appropriately licensed physical buyer offers; financing schedules; forwarder and warehouse quotations; and independent methodological criticism. Please first start a conversation about the evidence and intended permissions. Do not send confidential farm identities, transaction documents, personal data or proprietary exchange feeds without an agreed sharing process.

Research and product status

Current status: working public calculator and expanded methodology draft. Outstanding: representative empirical calibration, reviewed market-data licensing, integrated transaction back-testing, independent review and commercial acceptance testing. We explicitly distinguish measured evidence, user observations, external indicators and illustrative assumptions.
