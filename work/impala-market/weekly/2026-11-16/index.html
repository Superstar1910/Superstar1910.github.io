Impala Market Weekly: 16 November 2026

The shilling has stalled after the Bank of Uganda's emergency rate hike: banks quoted 4,105/4,115 per dollar on 12 November, between 4,040/4,050 a week earlier and 4,345/4,355 on 29 October. Bond yields have repriced sharply, with the 25-year at 18.475%, and inflation risk is rising.

Rates

The Bank of Uganda held the Central Bank Rate at 12% on 9 November and widened the corridor around it from 2 to 3 percentage points. It said the 225 basis point jump on 14 October needs time to work, after an emergency move from 9.75%.

The pressure is on prices. Food inflation rose to 10.7% in October from 4.5%, and the central bank now projects headline inflation of 7% to 7.5% over the next 12 months, against a 5% target.

At the 28 October auction the central bank accepted 18.475% on the 25-year, 17.925% on the 15-year, 16.8% on the 5-year and 16% on the 2-year. Bids were about Shs2.99tn against Shs1.4tn offered; it accepted about Shs1.88tn, roughly Shs933bn of it in the 25-year alone. The 25-year cleared 222.5 basis points above its 23 September level and the 2-year 400 basis points above. The gap between the 2-year and the 25-year was 247.5 basis points (our arithmetic from those yields). The 15-year was a new issue, so comparisons are between benchmark maturities, not identical bonds.

Shilling

Commercial banks quoted the shilling at 4,105/4,115 on 12 November, weaker than 4,040/4,050 a week earlier, which itself was a recovery from 4,345/4,355 on 29 October. Reuters reports the shilling crashed through repeated all-time lows in October before the emergency hike helped stabilise the market. One trader expects it to trade between 4,050 and 4,150 in the coming days, helped by remittance inflows. The governor told parliament on 10 November that offshore investors are returning.

The drivers on our FX Pressure Watch page, such as oil prices, corporate dollar demand and export receipts, still apply. Its Uganda snapshot is dated 21 September, when the shilling was near 3,930, so read its levels as historical.

What would change the picture: oil moving back above US$95 a barrel, weaker dollar inflows, or a further inflation surprise.

Equities

Umeme. Shareholders endorsed no final dividend for 2025 at the August AGM, after a net loss of Shs223.6bn. The buyout claim is still in London arbitration, with no announced timetable. Our coverage note still needs a cash bridge from the latest accounts before any valuation is published.
