## 1. Purpose and scope
The firm supports responsible AI adoption to improve professional quality, efficiency, accessibility and client outcomes. This policy applies to partners, employees, consultants and contractors using generative AI, machine learning, predictive models, embedded AI features or autonomous agents for firm work. **AI never displaces professional accountability.**

## 2. Principles
Professional accountability; client confidentiality; data protection; accuracy and verifiability; transparency where required; proportional human oversight; security; fairness; and evidence-based performance measurement.

## 3. Risk classification and approvals
| Risk | Illustrative use | Approval and safeguards |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Generic non-confidential brainstorming and formatting | Approved tool, trained user |
| Moderate | Internal research and non-sensitive first drafts | Manager approval, source checks |
| High | Legal opinions, client advice, valuations, regulated analysis, privileged or sensitive information | Engagement partner plus Risk/Compliance and AI owner; documented review |
| Prohibited | Unauthorised disclosure, fabricated citations or evidence, unapproved consequential decisions, autonomous actions beyond authority | Do not deploy |

Classify **both the activity and the data**; confidential data can make an otherwise routine task high-risk.

## 4. Acceptable use
Use approved systems only for authorised tasks, within client instructions, contracts and professional duties. Do not upload confidential, personal, privileged or regulated data to unapproved services; invent authorities or evidence; present unverified outputs as facts; or delegate binding client, regulatory, financial or legal actions to AI without documented authorisation.

## 5. Confidentiality, privacy and intellectual property
Apply data minimisation, role-based access, appropriate processing agreements, retention/deletion controls, transfer assessments and restrictions on vendor model training. Tool approval is not blanket approval for all data. Assess privilege, IP ownership and contractual restrictions independently.

## 6. Professional review and quality assurance
A competent professional must review consequential AI outputs before use. Verify primary sources, quotations and citations; independently recalculate material figures; inspect assumptions and completeness; check jurisdiction and current law; document reviewer approval for high-risk work. Review intensity must reflect consequences of error.

## 7. Agents and automation
Document owner, objective, permissions, data scope, approval gates, activity logs, escalation and kill switch. External messages, filings, transfers, contract execution and production-system changes require explicit authority and proportionate human controls.

## 8. Procurement and vendor controls
Assess security, data location, contractual terms, model training, reliability, auditability, integrations, resilience, exit rights, total cost and regulatory suitability before deployment. Maintain an approved AI systems and use-case register.

## 9. Training
Require induction for all users; role-specific training for partners, analysts, managers and technical operators; practical verification exercises; and periodic refreshers. Supervisors must understand delegation, professional development and quality gates.

## 10. Accountability
The board/partnership sets risk appetite. CEO/managing partner owns implementation. AI governance lead maintains registers and controls. Legal/Risk/Compliance validates professional and legal requirements. IT/Security approves technical controls. Engagement partners approve and supervise client work. Every user reports errors and incidents.

## 11. Incident management
Report suspected disclosure, fabricated material outputs, harmful decisions, security breaches or unauthorised agent actions promptly. Contain, preserve evidence, assess harm, notify clients/regulators when legally required, remediate and document lessons.

## 12. Monitoring, records and review
Track quality, rework, task time, review effort, costs, realised benefits, incidents and staff competency. **Hours saved are not automatically profits earned.** Retain proportionate records of approvals, assessments, incidents, training and material changes. Review at least annually.

## 13. Enforcement and approval
Violations may result in suspended access, retraining, discipline or regulatory escalation as appropriate. Effective only when formally approved by the firm.

## Operationalisation and team sensitisation
A policy is only effective when personnel understand and apply it. Firms should conduct a baseline inventory, tailor rules to client duties, approve tools, classify workflows, train staff using actual tasks, pilot quality controls, maintain an incident channel and review adoption metrics.
