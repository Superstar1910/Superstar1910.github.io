WRITING / AFRICA'S ENDURING ENTERPRISES

Africa's Enduring Enterprises: Evidence Update, 2 November 2026

A 104-year-old company, a disputed founding day and a change of controlling shareholder that is not yet complete.

Kenneth S. Legesi · 2 November 2026 · Original research / editorial

Why East African Breweries?

East African Breweries PLC (EABL) traces its origin to Kenya Breweries in 1922. The company subsequently became public, expanded across East Africa and developed management institutions that have outlived its founders. Its latest test is a prospective change of controlling shareholder from Diageo to Asahi. This is a case about institutional continuity—not a claim that the company has been indigenous African-owned for 104 years.

What is documented—and what remains unresolved?

1922 origin, but disputed day. EABL's heritage history records incorporation on 8 September 1922 and the first beer on 14 December. Asahi's December 2025 transaction announcement instead lists the company's establishment as 8 December 1922. The year is supported by both; the precise legal date must be reconciled against the original incorporation certificate.

Corporate transformation. EABL's history records a merger and name change in 1936, a Nairobi listing in 1954 and majority control by Diageo in 2000. The 2023 integrated report records Diageo increasing its aggregate interest to 65%. These are issuer disclosures; the underlying historical legal instruments still warrant checking.

Management succession. Jane Karuku's official biography confirms that she became Group Managing Director and CEO on 1 January 2021, after leading Kenya Breweries. This is an observable professional management transition; it does not establish that any single governance practice caused the company's longevity.

Ownership transition—not yet a verified closing. On 17 December 2025, Diageo announced agreements for Asahi to acquire its indirect 65% EABL interest, alongside a Kenyan spirits shareholding. Diageo estimated US$2.3 billion in net proceeds after tax and transaction costs and an implied US$4.8 billion enterprise value for EABL. These are seller proceeds, not a US$2.3 billion primary capital injection into EABL. Reuters reported Kenyan conditional merger approval on 10 September 2026. In its 2026 interim investor Q&A, Asahi still described outstanding approvals and anticipated closing in the second half of 2026. We found no definitive primary-source closing confirmation by 2 November; therefore we do not describe the transaction as completed.

Feature outline: What survives when owners change?

  1. Founders and the original legal entity: reconcile 1922 incorporation and the transition to the EABL name.
  2. Capital-market institution: trace listing, minority shareholders, subsidiaries and long-term funding.
  3. Professional succession: test the depth of management capability beyond the founder generation.
  4. Control versus capital formation: separate secondary shareholder sale proceeds from new investment into the operating business.
  5. Post-closing continuity: monitor board appointments, minority protections, licensing, local brands, supplier relationships and future capital expenditure.

The institutional lesson is a hypothesis worth testing: enduring enterprises preserve capabilities that can be transferred across generations of owners. The research cannot yet demonstrate that EABL's governance caused its longevity.
