WRITING / AFRICA'S ENDURING ENTERPRISES / RESEARCH DISPATCH

Eight decades, a century-old lineage, and the problem of institutional identity

Nigerian Breweries, New Kenya Co-operative Creameries and Ethiopian Airlines show why a founding year is only the beginning of the investigation.

Kenneth S. Legesi · 26 October 2026 · Research status: provisional evidence update, not a definitive longevity ranking

Three developments worth recording

Nigerian Breweries turns 80. The company dates its incorporation to 1946 and marked the milestone with an anniversary campaign in September 2026. Its founding arrangement involved UAC and HEINEKEN, making this an example of a Nigeria-incorporated enterprise—not evidence of indigenous Nigerian founding. The company reports that its shares began trading on 5 September 1973. Anniversary history · Investor relations.

More revealing than the anniversary is the governance record: company disclosures put HEINEKEN's shareholding at 56.13% in September 2020 and 72.9% in December 2025. That is a 16.77 percentage-point increase, but the precise transactions behind it still need reconciliation. An exchange-filed notification dated 15 May 2025 records Hans Essaadi's departure on 30 June and Thibaut Boidin's appointment from 1 July 2025. It also describes a NGN 550 billion rights issue; the allotment and dilution history need separate examination.

New KCC carries a 101-year lineage—but is not a 101-year-old legal company. The original Kenya Co-operative Creameries is dated to 1925 in the Kenya Yearbook. Yet the company's Treasury-hosted annual financial statements record a new cooperative registered on 24 June 2003 and a distinct company incorporated on 19 November 2004 with 100% Government of Kenya ownership. Brand and asset lineage survived; the legal entity and ownership form changed. This distinction is central to any credible list of Africa's oldest companies and cooperatives.

Ethiopian Airlines marked 80 years of flying on 8 April 2026. Its official overview separates 21 December 1945 (founding) from 8 April 1946 (first operations) and identifies the Ethiopian government as the sole owner. The 8 April 2026 announcement marks the anniversary of operations, not the original founding date.

Selected feature under development

Who Owns an 80-Year-Old Company? Nigerian Breweries and the Institutions Beyond Its Founders

Thesis: Eight decades of existence do not tell us who founded an enterprise, who now controls it, or which governance systems enabled successive managers to keep it operating.

  1. Founding versus nationality: the 1946 UAC–HEINEKEN arrangement and why Nigerian incorporation differs from indigenous founding.
  2. Capital-market institution: the 1973 listing, later acquisitions and the ability to raise new equity.
  3. Ownership change: HEINEKEN's reported 56.13% and 72.9% stakes, with transaction-level reconciliation still required.
  4. Succession: the July 2025 chief executive transition and the board's role in leadership renewal.
  5. What actually endured: test whether governance, capital access and management systems explain survival rather than simply assuming that they do.

Evidence boundary: 1946 is company-reported, but the original incorporation certificate and exact date have not been obtained. The 2003/2004 New KCC transitions are documented in official financial statements; the 1925 predecessor date comes from a government historical account. Neither ownership concentration nor executive succession alone proves causal drivers of survival.

Research agenda

The next documents to secure are Nigerian Breweries' original incorporation certificate, complete 2024 rights-issue allotment and post-issue shareholder register, and New KCC's original predecessor registration and transfer instruments. The public candidate directory is a provisional evidence register, not a ranking of legally continuous centenarian firms.
