Bloomberg by Bloomberg — Some Lessons

Original publication ↗

I was recently asked to be on the Board of Advisors for a media and data analytics startup. The pitch was we are Company X and we want to tell the story of African startups to i) enable others both locally and globally to know about the startup ecosystem in Africa, bridge the knowledge gap and information asymmetry, in the process bring down the risk premium attributed to investing on the continent and in startups. and ii) while we are doing this, we would like to build a successful media, data and analytics business.

The African culture has always been one of story telling, we have always told stories as a way of sharing experiences, sharing knowledge, sharing feelings and attitudes. The experiences, knowledge, feelings and attitudes needed to make or break a business in the African setting have to be passed on within our communities. This shall help build an African business culture suited to our own environment. I believe this is the right thing to do. The company will not only be telling stories of African businesses but will also be building a data and information business around and about African businesses, for two reasons mainly; firstly to share knowledge with the local ecosystem to inform the right decision making, share successes and failures and secondly to help inform, especially those investing in the startup scene in Africa, to reduce information asymmetry and the associated risk premium placed on Africa owing to from a lack of information.

To bridge my own knowledge and information gap about a media and data businesses, I have been reading and speaking to people in the Industry. For those I have transgressed, please do forgive me for my pestilence. In a continued quest for knowledge, one of the books I have been reading is ‘Bloomberg by Bloomberg’. When I came across this book, I thought who better to learn from than the man himself. I have learnt some practical tips on business and life in the process. I share below some lessons based on quotes and extracts from Bloomberg by Bloomberg:

  1. Confirmatory bias: “We just did what all great sales people do: We presented everything we had, and then highlighted whatever facts enabled customers to convince themselves they were getting a good deal.” people buy what is familiar and or everyone else is buying.
  2. Lockin: “Never let the customer go down the street to another store — he might find they sell the same thing there and not come back”
  3. Unique Selling Point (USP): Know your USP and focus your activities towards delivering it: “We think timely, accurate news stories that are well researched and well written will be in demand for a long time. Technology will continuously revolutionize distribution. But our product is content. It remains consistent”
  4. High demand, low supply: “sell something no one else has, buyers will have little choice”
  5. Consistency and self — improvement: “just growing a product isn’t enough to make it succeed, though. Infrastructure must expand too. Backup, contingency planning, reliability — all become even more important than new offerings. The longer you have clients, the more they depend on you. The more clients you have the more you have to lose. The more you are viewed as the establishment, the more the old reliable reputation is worth.”

Now to do as we learn. As I learn more from my mistakes (experiences), I hope I can continue to share. In the meantime, I am taking any recommendations on further books to read and advice on how to make a media and data business successful. Suggestions are welcome!
