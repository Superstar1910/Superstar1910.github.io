Carbon Markets in East Africa

Learning in Public, Building with Purpose

At the start of this year, I made a commitment to myself and my network—to learn deeply, build thoughtfully, and share openly in the areas that matter most to me and the future I want to help shape.

That commitment revolves around five themes:

  1. Blended Finance – including climate finance and carbon markets

  2. Personal Investing – across asset classes, with a long-term lens

  3. AI and Frontier Technologies – understanding tools that shape how we build and decide

  4. SME Finance – bridging the capital access gap for entrepreneurs in emerging markets

  5. Personal Growth – resilience, stewardship, and purposeful leadership

This series is part of that learning journey—rooted in climate finance, with a focus on carbon markets in East Africa, where opportunity and uncertainty are rising in equal measure.

Making Sense of a Complex, Emerging Market

The idea of carbon markets is everywhere—but the details are fuzzy for most people, including those working in climate, policy, or investment.

In East Africa, countries like Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, and the DRC are being positioned as suppliers of high-quality carbon credits to global buyers. Forests, wetlands, savannahs, cookstove projects, clean energy, and regenerative farming are all part of the growing pipeline.

But:

  • Are we ready?

  • Who benefits?

  • What risks are we taking on as countries, as communities, as entrepreneurs?

  • Can carbon credits be a source of transformational finance—or just another extractive mechanism in green clothing?

This 10-part article series is an attempt to unpack those questions—and to look at both the promise and pitfalls of carbon markets in our region.

From Research to Writing to Reflection

I’m writing this series as part of my own process: learning through research, reflection, conversation, and practice. I’ve been fortunate to work across investing, development finance, and ecosystem building in Africa, and I increasingly see carbon markets as a place where those threads intersect—with real potential for impact.

The articles draw on current data, policy trends, real projects, and first-hand insights. They follow a structured approach—to make them useful, readable, and relevant for practitioners, investors, developers, and policymakers alike.

Each article stands alone, but together they paint a picture of a region in transition—full of risk, reward, and responsibility.
