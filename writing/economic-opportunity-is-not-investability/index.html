Economic Opportunity Is Not Investability

Kenneth S. Legesi, CFA · 28 October 2026 · Foundational essay

The existence of an investment opportunity does not establish that capital can be deployed, protected or recovered on workable terms.

Africa is often presented through the language of potential: expanding markets, demographic growth, mineral wealth, urbanisation and unmet demand. These may all point to opportunities for enterprise. But the existence of an opportunity is not the same as its investability.

An investor does not commit capital to a demographic projection. Capital is committed through a legal and financial arrangement, in a currency, within a jurisdiction, to an enterprise or asset whose future cash flows remain uncertain. The investor must be able to enter, finance, govern, protect and ultimately realise the investment. Each of those steps depends on institutions and market arrangements that growth statistics alone do not capture.

From potential to executable capital

Consider two economies with similarly promising demand. In one, foreign exchange is accessible, contracts are enforceable, financing is available for appropriate tenors and investors can exit through credible mechanisms. In the other, financing is short-term, currency conversion is unpredictable and remedies for non-payment are difficult to enforce. Their economic potential may look comparable while the conditions for investment differ materially.

This does not imply that difficult markets are uninvestable, or that stronger institutions guarantee superior returns. Investors may earn high returns by structuring around constraints, bearing risks others avoid or accessing opportunities unavailable to competitors. It does mean that opportunity and the mechanisms that convert opportunity into recoverable capital deserve separate analysis.

Five provisional dimensions

The Africa Investability Index (AII) research programme begins with five candidate dimensions: political and institutional durability; domestic capital formation; market accessibility and capital mobility; enterprise durability; and capital protection and recovery.

These dimensions are not yet an index, and their importance has not been empirically established. They form a set of hypotheses about the conditions under which productive capital can be mobilised and sustained. The research will test whether they are distinct, measurable and useful beyond established country-risk and governance frameworks.

Whose investability?

Investability is not necessarily a single national characteristic. A pension fund purchasing local-currency government bonds, a venture investor backing a technology business and an infrastructure lender financing a power project face different constraints. Country-level conditions matter, but transaction structure, asset class, investor eligibility and legal remedies may matter just as much.

The programme therefore distinguishes ex-ante investability—conditions observable before capital is committed—from transaction feasibility and ex-post investment outcomes, including recoveries, exits and realised returns. This separation matters: an index cannot credibly claim to predict investment outcomes if those outcomes are built into the same explanatory score.

What the research must prove

The ambition is not to publish another league table of African countries. It is to test whether a disciplined account of institutional and financial mechanisms adds information that investors and policymakers do not already obtain from growth, governance, sovereign-risk or financial-development indicators.

The programme will examine competing methods, including a multidimensional dashboard rather than a single composite ranking. It will prioritise transparent definitions, data provenance, comparisons against existing benchmarks and out-of-time validation. Where data are missing or the evidence is inconclusive, the limitations should remain visible.

Economic opportunity is the starting point. Investability concerns the conditions under which that opportunity can become a durable financial claim. Understanding the difference is the research agenda.

Research status: This essay introduces a working conceptual framework. It does not report validated AII country scores or investment recommendations. The underlying research repository is private; publication of methods and findings will follow source review and editorial approval.
