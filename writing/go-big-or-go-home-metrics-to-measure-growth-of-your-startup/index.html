Go big or go home — metrics to measure growth of your startup

Original publication ↗

For a startup business to succeed, key DNA markers that investors look for are a good idea, clear product/market fit, founder focus and tenacity, team alignment and fit. However, all this counts for nought if this DNA does not translate into growth. Investors care about growth more than everything else as growth translates into return on their investment and an increasing valuation. Successive rounds of seed and venture capital funding shall be premised on hitting growth, scale and traction numbers, no down rounds please!

Traction is evidence that your product or service has started that “hockey- stick” adoption rate which implies a large market, a valid business model and sustainable growth.

Scaling, which is different from growth means increasing revenue at a lower additional cost. When businesses grow, they are able to increase revenue with proportionate additional costs while scale means a business is able to increase revenue with no or lower additional costs. This is reflected in not only increasing sales but also increasing profit margin ie the proportion of revenue kept as profit. The proof in scaling is the widening gap over time between gross margin (net revenue) and operating expenses.

Startups should thus be concerned with how to scale and build traction. To be able to scale and build traction, it is important to understand the levers (and metrics) that are used to measure scaling and traction, taking an example of a platform type business that is driven by users completing transactions think a telecom operator, mobile app or an online/mobile market place. Typical metrics that would be used to measure and gauge scaling and tractions relate to the value proposition journey that results in revenue:

  1. Acquisition (no of new users/customers): How many new users are being acquired every day/week/month/year; also note the cost of acquiring these users aka Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC).
  2. Activation (no of users completing their 1st transaction): On acquisition of a user, it is important that they are able and triggered to make their first transaction. It is common for one to acquire customers for example through app downloads, however, they may not go on to make transactions and even delete the application.
  3. Retention (number of users completing a transaction in the recent past, say 1 week): To retain customers, they should be encouraged to keep completing transactions and this is measured through the recent transactions completed.
  4. Revenue (gross transactions volume and value per user): The metrics above translate into revenue or not through number of users multiplied by transaction size. The aim is to have transactions volume growing but more importantly value of the business by keeping as much of the revenue generated as possible.

As a startup founder or investor, these metrics should be on one’s fingertips and monitored constantly to gauge health and growth of the business.
