I Hope This Helps Someone Today: How to Safely Hold Your Bitcoin

Originally published on Substack: 2025-10-10 · Original publication ↗

The future is decentralised. The future is now. The future is you.

Visual designed with https://drawit.chamuka.ai/, an excellent tool for creating smart visuals and publishing layouts

Every so often, someone asks me:

“Kenneth, I just bought some Bitcoin. Which wallet should I use?”

And every time, I smile — because that question says something powerful:
we’re finally moving toward a world where people are taking ownership of their own value.
The future is decentralised, and the future is now.

But here’s the truth no one tells you: where you keep your crypto matters as much as what you buy.

🔐 If You Hold the Keys, You Hold the Power

If you’ve got an appreciable amount of crypt (I set this threshold at $5,000 or more) and you’re planning to buy and hold BTC or other crypto for the long term, your safest path is self-custody — owning your keys, not trusting anyone else to hold them.

That means using a hardware (cold) wallet like Trezor or Ledger.
These devices keep your Bitcoin offline — untouched by hackers, government bans, or exchange shutdowns.

It’s the purest form of financial freedom.
But it comes with a responsibility: if you lose your secret phrase or private keys, no one can help you recover it.
No password reset. No “Forgot your PIN?”.

That’s the price — and privilege — of sovereignty.

When Convenience Matters: Hot Wallets

If you want quick access on your phone or browser, but still want control, go for a non-custodial hot wallet.

My go-tos:

  • Trust Wallet — easy, popular, multi-chain

  • Cake Wallet — private, supports BTC & Monero

You still own your keys, so no one can freeze your funds.
But remember: if you lose your recovery phrase, that’s it. There’s no helpline.

If You Prefer Simplicity (and a Safety Net)

If self-custody feels daunting, or you just want a password-reset option, use a reputable centralised exchange like:

  • Binance

  • Coinbase

  • Kraken

They’ll hold your BTC for you, manage backups, and let you recover your account if something goes wrong.

The catch?
You don’t actually own the coins — they do.
And if something happens (a hack, insolvency, or regulatory freeze), your funds might be locked.

It’s the old crypto truth:

“Not your keys, not your coins.”

For Quick Local Use

If you just want to buy and move small amounts quickly — maybe to test the waters — you can use regional apps like Chipper Cash.

They’re convenient and integrate with mobile money, but they don’t publish proof of reserves or independent audits.
So, I wouldn’t hold large amounts there long-term.

Bitcoin-Only Simplicity

If you want a wallet that’s purely for Bitcoin, nothing else, check out the community list on Bitcoin.org.
It’s a good place to start if you’re minimalist or security-focused.

My Takeaway

Why This Matters

In a world of collapsing currencies and financial intermediaries, decentralisation isn’t just a trend — it’s a mindset.
It’s about trust without permission.
About ownership without dependence.
About risk as a doorway to freedom.

So if you’re buying Bitcoin today, don’t just think about how much you have — think about where and how you hold it.

Self-custody isn’t for everyone. It’s not meant to be.
But for those ready to take that step, it’s one of the most liberating moves you’ll ever make.
