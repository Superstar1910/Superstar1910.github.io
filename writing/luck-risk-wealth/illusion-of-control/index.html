Are You Really in Control?

A financial plan becomes fragile when it assumes the world will behave exactly as expected.

The question behind the outcome

We control some inputs: spending decisions, savings habits, research standards and the size of the risks we take. We influence others: earning power, professional reputation and opportunities. Market returns, political events and the timing of illness or recession remain outside our control.

What makes this difficult

The illusion of control appears when forecasts are treated as promises. A spreadsheet can calculate a precise future balance, but its precision depends on uncertain assumptions. Even the best model cannot abolish uncertainty.

A more useful way forward

Separate your financial decisions into control, influence and acceptance. Build liquidity, diversify where appropriate and test what happens if income falls or investment returns disappoint. A robust plan has room for surprise.

Field note: Aim for resilience rather than perfect prediction.

What would you change in your own financial decisions after examining this idea? The point of this series is not to offer a shortcut to wealth, but to make the trade-offs clearer and the decisions more deliberate.
