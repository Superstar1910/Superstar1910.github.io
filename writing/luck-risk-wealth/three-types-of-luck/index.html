The Three Types of Luck

Luck is not a single mysterious event. It shapes the starting hand, the opportunities we encounter and the results we receive.

The question behind the outcome

Constitutive luck concerns characteristics and circumstances we did not choose, including the family and environment into which we were born. Circumstantial luck concerns encounters and conditions: a mentor, a market opening or a timely introduction. Outcome luck concerns what happens after a decision, when uncertainty still remains.

What makes this difficult

These categories overlap. An investor may make a well-researched decision yet experience an adverse outcome; another may profit from a weak thesis because a market rally arrived. The result alone does not reveal the quality of the decision.

A more useful way forward

Evaluate decisions with two separate questions: was the process sound given information available at the time, and what happened afterward? Keep a decision journal to separate skill from fortune.

Field note: Respect luck without surrendering agency. Good processes improve your chances, not your certainty.

What would you change in your own financial decisions after examining this idea? The point of this series is not to offer a shortcut to wealth, but to make the trade-offs clearer and the decisions more deliberate.
