Your Mind Can Make You Poorer

Investment decisions are made by people, not by spreadsheets alone.

The question behind the outcome

Fear of missing out can make a rising price look like evidence. Loss aversion can keep us attached to a failing investment. Anchoring can turn a purchase price into an irrelevant target. Herd behaviour can substitute social proof for analysis, while present bias rewards immediate comfort over distant goals.

What makes this difficult

None of these tendencies disappears because we know their names. Financial professionals experience them too. The practical defence is a repeatable decision process that slows down reactions and makes assumptions visible.

A more useful way forward

Before a significant decision, record the thesis, valuation or affordability logic, downside scenario and conditions that would change your mind. Use waiting periods for impulsive purchases and review outcomes without rewriting the original rationale.

Field note: Discipline is less about possessing a perfect temperament than building a process that anticipates imperfect judgement.

What would you change in your own financial decisions after examining this idea? The point of this series is not to offer a shortcut to wealth, but to make the trade-offs clearer and the decisions more deliberate.
