Macro-Themed Portfolios with ChatGPT: 2025 ETF Performance (as at Aug 8, 2025)

Originally published on Substack: 2025-09-05 · Original publication ↗

TLDR
At the start of 2025, I reviewed macro outlooks from major investment houses and used ChatGPT to turn their ideas into ETF-based portfolios - custom indexes reflecting each firm’s view. This article tracks how those portfolios have performed up to August 8, 2025, compares them to the S&P 500 and FTSE All-Share, highlights the key drivers of returns, and considers what adjustments may be needed for the rest of the year.

Goal: Build Investment Portfolios Using Global Insights and AI

Like many investors in Uganda or across Africa, I’ve often wondered: how do I invest beyond my local market? And more importantly, how do I make sense of all the information out there such as macroeconomics, market trends and forecasts?

So at the start of 2025, I set a goal: build global investment portfolios using real-world economic insights and tools, from Uganda.

To frame and guide the investing decision, I read the macro outlooks from the leaders in the field of investing and economics such as Bloomberg, JP Morgan, HSBC and summarised the outlook and implied themes for the year, translated these into investable ETF allocations. I used ChatGPT to do this.

Target: Convert Macro Ideas Into Real Portfolios

At the start of 2025, here’s what the different players were saying:

Bloomberg Outlook (Jan 2025)

  • Expected US growth to slow modestly but remain positive.

  • Saw opportunities in infrastructure spending, supported by US fiscal programs.

  • Anticipated tech resilience, particularly in AI, cloud computing and semiconductors.

  • Viewed China as a selective opportunity, especially in tech, if policy stimulus materialized.

  • Recommended a balanced allocation between growth sectors and investment-grade credit.

JP Morgan Outlook (Jan 2025)

  • Predicted a “soft landing” for the US economy.

  • Favored quality stocks globally, with a tilt toward developed markets.

  • Expected yields to stay elevated in the first half of the year, benefiting short-duration bonds.

  • Believed global real estate could see a modest recovery.

  • Recommended diversified portfolios combining equities, fixed income, and alternatives.

HSBC Outlook (Jan 2025)

  • Saw strong potential in emerging markets, supported by lower US dollar strength.

  • Expected infrastructure and renewable energy to lead growth.

  • Anticipated that China would stabilize but remain volatile.

  • Suggested overweight positions in innovation sectors like clean tech and biotech.

  • Favored a higher equity allocation versus bonds.

Trump Opportunities Fund (TOF) – Thematic Strategy

(Not a bank outlook, but a strategy I created from more speculative themes)

  • Heavy allocations to US defense contractors, oil and gas, and blockchain/crypto.

  • Expected defense spending to grow in response to geopolitical tensions.

  • Saw crypto as a hedge against monetary policy unpredictability.

Story: The Portfolios in Action

Step 1: Building the Portfolios

Using ChatGPT, I matched each outlook to ETFs that reflected those strategies to arrive at weights or percentages to allocate to each ETF for a given risk appetite (used a moderate risk investor across all strategies).

The prompt: “Here is the 2025 global macroeconomic/investment outlook from [Bloomberg/JP Morgan/HSBC/etc.]. Based on the themes in this outlook, suggest a diversified ETF portfolio that reflects their asset allocation view. Include equity, fixed income and thematic ETFs where relevant. Please provide ETF tickers, approximate weights, and a short explanation for each choice.”

Step 3: Risk-Adjusted Performance (Sharpe Ratio)

To assess the excess return per unit of risk, also defined as the Sharpe Ratio*

*Sharpe Ratio = return minus risk-free rate, divided by volatility.

By August 8, 2025, the Sharpe Ratios tell a clearer story than raw returns alone:

  • Bloomberg 2025 (Sharpe Ratio, 0.172) maintains the strongest risk-adjusted performance. Its steady exposure to US tech and infrastructure has delivered returns without excessive volatility.

  • JP Morgan 2025 (Sharpe Ratio, 0.115) sits in second place. Its balanced equity and bond mix has kept volatility in check, though upside is modest.

  • HSBC Growth 2025 (Sharpe Ratio, -0.020) has slipped into negative Sharpe territory. Emerging market and innovation themes have not compensated for the higher volatility so far.

  • TOF (Trump Opportunities Fund) (Sharpe Ratio, -0.210) remains highly volatile, and the Sharpe Ratio shows that the extra risk has not translated into meaningful returns.

  • S&P 500 (Sharpe Ratio, -0.150) surprisingly posts a negative Sharpe Ratio, reflecting subdued returns against relatively high volatility.

  • FTSE All-Share (Sharpe Ratio, 0.025) is just above break-even, showing modest risk-adjusted gains thanks to steadier performance from energy and financials.

Overall, this reinforces the value of diversified strategies like Bloomberg and JP Morgan versus concentrated or speculative approaches. Risk-adjusted measures show not just who is winning, but who is winning wisely.

Can You Do This from Uganda or Africa?

Absolutely, here is how:

  • Use brokers that give global exposure. I personally have experience with Interactive Brokers but others like Saxo, Bamboo, or Hisa also claim exposure to global securities for African users. The biggest hurdle is the AML/KYC especially showing proof of address (African problems).

  • Fund with USD (bank wire, forex, or even stablecoins).

  • Buy ETFs on US or UK stock exchanges.

You don’t need millions to start. A few hundred dollars, good strategy, and discipline go a long way.

So What is the Outlook for the Rest of 2025?

Bloomberg

  • Still positive on US tech and infrastructure.

  • More cautious on China given policy uncertainty.

  • Suggests maintaining investment-grade bonds as rates could fall in late 2025.

JP Morgan

  • Still sees a soft landing scenario ie the US economy would slow down but stay positive, markets could perform steadily.

  • Prefers high-quality equities and short-duration bonds.

  • Cautious on adding to Emerging Markets (EM) exposure until clearer signs of global growth.

HSBC

  • Keeps faith in EM and infrastructure themes.

  • Sees potential for China to rebound if fiscal stimulus increases.

  • Suggests trimming innovation-heavy allocations if volatility remains high - AI, Biotech and cleantech anyone?

TOF

  • Defense stocks still expected to outperform if geopolitical tensions rise.

  • Oil likely to remain volatile; energy allocation may need active rebalancing.

  • Crypto exposure remains speculative higher upside, higher risk.

What I’ve Learned

  • Bank and Investment team outlooks give a starting point.

  • ChatGPT is a powerful translator of macro theory into actionable portfolios - macro reports are complex and you need to digest them simply, allows comparison of the different outlooks easily. However, this does not replace the need for own due diligence or the need to update your portfolio as market conditions and context changes.

  • ETFs make execution simple from almost anywhere - easy access, flexible entry size for positions, instant diversification, transparency and liquidity as you invest.

  • African investors can and should think globally - why? relying only on local markets exposes them to concentrated risks like currency depreciation, political uncertainty, and sector dominance (often banks). Global investing through ETFs provides diversification across regions and industries, access to high-growth themes such as AI, clean tech, and semiconductors that aren’t available locally, and a hedge against weakening local currencies. With today’s tools—USD accounts, mobile FX apps, and international brokers—it’s easier than ever for African investors to preserve wealth and participate in the same opportunities that drive returns in global markets.
