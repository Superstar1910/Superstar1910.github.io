Solar Investment in Uganda: Policies, Incentives, and Market Response

Originally published on Substack: 2025-04-11 · Original publication ↗

Introduction

Uganda presents an interesting opportunity for solar energy investment, with abundant sunlight, a growing demand for electricity, and government-backed incentives to support renewable energy expansion. For an investor interested in solar power development, Uganda offers both grid-connected and off-grid investment opportunities, particularly in rural electrification and commercial solar solutions.

If you’ve been thinking about investing in solar power in Uganda, here’s what you need to know about the policy environment, tax incentives, market trends, and response to date.

Uganda’s Solar Market: A Snapshot

Uganda sits in the solar belt and receives an average of 5.1 kWh/m²/day of solar radiation¹, making it one of the best locations for solar power generation in Africa. However, despite this potential, solar energy still accounts for a small share of the national electricity mix.

  • Total Installed Electricity Capacity (2024): 2,048.1 MW, with solar contributing ~54 MW to the grid².

  • Electricity Access: 28% of the population is connected to the grid. Another 28% relies on off-grid solar solutions (PAYGo home systems, solar mini-grids)³.

  • Annual Electricity Demand Growth: 14%, projected to increase per capita consumption from 80 kWh today to 1,750 kWh by 2050⁴.

Clearly, solar is underutilized but holds immense potential, especially for off-grid households, small businesses, and commercial users.

Government Initiatives & Policies Driving Solar Growth

Uganda’s government has made renewable energy development a priority, and solar energy features prominently in national policies:

1. Energy Transition Plan (ETP) – 2023

  • Targets universal electricity access by 2030 and net-zero energy emissions by 2065⁵.

  • Requires $8 billion in clean energy investments annually over the next decade.

  • Focuses on expanding solar and other low-emission electricity sources.

2. Electricity Connections Policy (ECP)

  • Aims to add 300,000 new connections annually, with a third coming from off-grid solar solutions⁶.

3. GET FiT Solar Facility (2014)

  • A donor-funded initiative that subsidized solar projects to lower generation costs and boost solar adoption⁷.

4. Renewable Energy Policy (2007)

  • Provides tax exemptions, grants, and financing schemes to promote solar energy investments⁸.

Tax Incentives & Financial Support for Solar Investments

For an investor, the Ugandan government offers several financial incentives to encourage solar energy adoption:

1. VAT Exemptions on Solar Equipment

  • Zero VAT on solar panels, inverters, batteries, and solar water heaters⁹.

  • Tax-free imports of specialized solar equipment to reduce costs.

2. Import Duty Exemptions

  • No import duty on solar energy equipment and components.

3. Investment Capital Allowances

  • Investors can deduct capital expenditures on solar projects to reduce taxable income¹⁰.

4. Feed-in Tariffs (FiTs)

  • Provides guaranteed payments per kWh for electricity generated from solar projects¹¹.

  • Designed to create a stable revenue stream for independent solar producers.

PAYGo Solar: The Growing Off-Grid Market

One of the most exciting solar opportunities in Uganda is the Pay-As-You-Go (PAYGo) model, which has made solar energy accessible to rural and off-grid communities.

Key Players in Uganda’s PAYGo Market:

  • M-KOPA Solar – Leading PAYGo provider in East Africa (500,000+ customers)¹².

  • d.light – Serves 30M homes across 72 countries.

  • SolarNow – Offers SHS, solar appliances, and solar water pumps.

  • Fenix International (ENGIE Energy Access) – Focuses on PAYGo ReadyPay solar solutions.

PAYGo Pricing Estimates:

  • Basic SHS: $100–$200, with payments as low as $0.50/day over 1–2 years¹³.

  • Larger systems (TVs, refrigerators): $300–$500, with flexible payment plans.

Large-Scale Solar Projects & Investment Opportunities

Uganda has several commercial solar projects in various stages of development:

  • Xsabo Lira Solarline (50 MW, $45M investment) – Expected completion Dec 2024¹⁴.

  • Xsabo Nkonge Solarline (20 MW, $22M investment) – Expected completion June 2024.

  • Ituka Solar Power Station (24 MW, $19M investment) – Expected completion 2025.

Investment Options:

  1. Utility-Scale Solar Farms – Investing in large grid-connected projects.

  2. Off-Grid Solar Ventures – Partnering with PAYGo companies to expand solar access.

  3. Solar Equipment Distribution – Local assembly and supply chains to reduce costs.

  4. Solar for Productive Use – Funding solar-powered irrigation, cold storage, and microgrids.
