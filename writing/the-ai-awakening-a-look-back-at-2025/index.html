The AI Awakening: A Look Back at 2025 and Navigating the Embodied Future of 2026

Originally published on Substack: 2026-03-22 · Original publication ↗

As the calendar turns, the investment world once again pivots to the perennial pastime of predictions. Yet, in the realm of Artificial Intelligence, this isn’t mere speculation; it’s an urgent necessity. The past year, 2025, has been a whirlwind of foundational shifts. We believe 2026 will be the true inflection point, moving AI from our screens into our physical world. For investors, understanding this transition is key to separating genuine growth from speculative froth in what many are already calling an “AI bubble.”

2025: The Year AI Got Its “Voice” and “Vision” (and Some Growing Pains)

Looking back at 2025, we witnessed a profound deepening of AI’s capabilities. Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) evolved from impressive chatbots into genuinely intelligent assistants, capable of nuanced reasoning, complex code generation, and sophisticated content creation. AI wasn’t just understanding language; it was understanding context and beginning to generate media (images, video, music) with remarkable fidelity.

Key Observations from 2025:

  • The “Model Wars” Intensified: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta all pushed the boundaries of their flagship models. The race for ever-larger, more capable general-purpose AI became a defining theme.

  • Early Vertical Integration: We saw the first clear signs of companies attempting to own the entire AI stack – from custom silicon and vast data centers to proprietary models and end-user applications.

  • Compute Demand Skyrocketed (Again): The hunger for GPUs, especially NVIDIA’s, continued unabated, leading to unprecedented capital expenditures by hyperscalers.

  • The Open-Source Surge: While proprietary models grabbed headlines, the open-source community demonstrated remarkable progress, with models approaching or even surpassing closed-source capabilities for many enterprise tasks. This set the stage for crucial shifts in deployment.

However, 2025 also highlighted the AI bubble anxieties. Valuations for pure-play AI companies reached dizzying heights, often disconnected from immediate revenue realities. Questions around sustainability, intellectual property, and the sheer cost of training these massive models began to surface.

2026: The Year AI Gets a “Body” – And How to Position for It

Our core thesis for 2026 is unambiguous: This is the breakout year for Embodied AI and advanced robotics. We will move beyond sophisticated software into a world where AI-powered physical agents begin to perform complex, adaptive tasks in real-world environments. This transition will redefine industries and create new investment opportunities, but it demands a strategic approach to avoid the pitfalls of overvalued speculation.

Key Predictions for 2026:

  1. Embodied AI & Robotics Become Mainstream: The fusion of advanced LMMs with robotics will move from research labs to industrial deployment. Humanoid robots will transition from prototypes to pre-production units, with companies like Tesla making significant strides in scaling their Optimus program for real-world applications. Expect to see adaptive, intelligent robots in manufacturing, logistics, and perhaps even early consumer service roles.

  2. NVIDIA’s Sustained Dominance, But a Shift in Focus: While NVIDIA remains indispensable, the emphasis will shift from pure Training to pervasive Inference. As millions of robots and autonomous systems go live, the demand for real-time, energy-efficient AI processing at the edge will explode. NVIDIA’s next-generation architectures (likely the “Rubin” platform) will be designed with this inference scale in mind.

  3. Open Models Win the “Last Mile”: For Embodied AI, latency, privacy, and customization are paramount. Open-weight models, fine-tuned on specialized datasets, will often be preferred for on-device deployment in robotics. This enables rapid iteration, security, and cost-effectiveness that closed cloud-based models struggle to match in real-time physical interactions.

  4. Accelerated Vertical Integration: The race to own the “full stack” will intensify. Companies with vast data, proprietary silicon, sophisticated models, and clear deployment strategies (especially in robotics) will consolidate power. We anticipate more strategic mergers or deep vertical integration between compute providers, data aggregators, and model developers, as firms seek to control their destiny and reduce reliance on third-party bottlenecks.

Navigating the AI Bubble: Investment Positioning for 2026

For both retail and institutional investors, the goal is clear: Invest in the foundational enablers and the genuine beneficiaries of Embodied AI, not just the fleeting hype.

Strategic Buys for 2026:

  • The “Body Builders” (Embodied AI & Robotics):

    • Tesla (TSLA): A high-conviction bet on humanoid robotics. If Optimus Gen 3 achieves production scale and proves capable, Tesla’s valuation could undergo a profound re-rating. This is a speculative but potentially transformative play.

    • Teradyne (TER): A less glamorous but highly essential play on industrial robotics. Their Universal Robots division is a leader in collaborative robots (cobots), a segment set to benefit directly from AI integration.

    • Intuitive Surgical (ISRG): The leader in medical robotics. As AI enables greater autonomy, ISRG’s systems will expand their capabilities, driving deeper penetration in healthcare.

  • The “Brains & Nerves” (Compute & Inference Infrastructure):

    • NVIDIA (NVDA): While already a high-flyer, NVIDIA’s continued dominance in inference processing for edge devices and specialized AI data centers is undeniable. Look for their “Omniverse” platform to become critical for simulating robotic environments.

    • Broadcom (AVGO): A key beneficiary of vertical integration. As hyperscalers and tech giants design their own custom AI chips (ASICs), Broadcom’s expertise in ASIC development and networking solutions makes them indispensable.

    • Arm Holdings (ARM): The architecture for efficient edge AI. As robotics and specialized devices demand more processing with less power, Arm’s licensing model will ensure they capture a piece of every intelligent physical agent.

  • The “Full-Stack Integrators” (Vertical Plays):

    • Alphabet (GOOGL): Arguably the most vertically integrated AI powerhouse. With Gemini models, Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), DeepMind’s robotics research, and Waymo’s autonomous driving, Google is uniquely positioned to capitalize on embodied AI from end-to-end.

    • Amazon (AMZN): AWS powers vast swaths of AI, and their internal robotics operations are immense. Their continued investment in custom AI silicon (Trainium/Inferentia) and edge solutions positions them well.

    • Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM): The undisputed manufacturing backbone for all advanced AI chips. Geopolitical risks aside, TSM’s foundry leadership makes it a non-negotiable component of any AI portfolio.

    • Vertiv Holdings (VRT): An essential infrastructure play. The intense heat generated by next-gen AI chips demands advanced cooling solutions, and Vertiv is a leader in liquid cooling for data centers.

Hold (Watch for Entry/Catalysts):

  • AMD (AMD): A strong challenger in the AI accelerator market, especially for open-source ecosystems. Watch for clearer signs of market share gains against NVIDIA beyond specific niches.

  • Palantir (PLTR): Their Ontology platform is critical for integrating complex data, which will be vital for managing fleets of embodied AI. Look for accelerated adoption in industrial and defense sectors.

Avoid (High Hype/Lack of Clear Path):

  • Be wary of small-cap “AI” companies with vague roadmaps or those solely focused on consumer-facing LLM applications without a clear path to profitability or unique IP. The consolidation in 2026 will likely squeeze out many smaller players.

Conclusion: A Transformative Year Ahead

2026 is poised to be more than just another year for AI; it’s the year AI truly steps into the physical world. While the “AI bubble” remains a valid concern, strategic investors who focus on the foundational technologies, essential infrastructure, and the companies directly building and deploying Embodied AI will be best positioned to capture the long-term value creation.

We will revisit this article throughout 2026 to track these predictions and adjust our positioning as the future of AI unfolds. The journey from digital intelligence to physical embodiment is just beginning, and the opportunities are immense for those who understand the shift.
