The AI Bubble: Why Manias Never Really Die (Part 2)

Originally published on Substack: 2025-12-30 · Original publication ↗

The Real Question Isn’t If It’s a Bubble. It’s How to Live Through One

Every generation finds its obsession, a narrative so potent that logic simply cannot compete with imagination.

For our parents, it was the fiber-optic cables and dot-coms of the 90s. For us, it is Artificial Intelligence.

The debate is no longer about whether AI will change the world; that consensus is already settled. The real challenge for the investor is surviving the “In-Between.” This is the feverish phase where capital outpaces proof, where supply cannot track demand and where the future is priced in decades before it arrives.

The Eye of the Storm: A Capital Cycle Without Precedent

We are currently in the eye of the most powerful capital formation cycle since the birth of the internet. This isn’t just a software boom; it is a physical rebuilding of the global economy’s engine. The signals are no longer anecdotal—they are measurable in hundreds of billions of dollars.

  • Nvidia’s $500 Billion Bottleneck: The transition to the Blackwell Ultra (B300) architecture has created a “supply-constrained” environment that defies standard cyclicality. As of December 2025, Nvidia holds a staggering $500 billion backlog for the 2025–2026 period, with next-generation units effectively sold out through the next four quarters [1][2].

  • ASML and the Lithography Ceiling: The bottleneck of the entire revolution sits in Veldhoven. ASML’s order backlog remains near record highs at €36–38 billion [3]. With production of High-NA EUV machines limited to roughly 20 units annually, the queue for 2nm-ready hardware now extends into 2027 [4].

  • The $400 Billion Arms Race: The “Big Four”—Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta—have unveiled 2025 capital budgets that eclipse the GDP of most nations. Combined annual Capex is projected to hit $405 billion this year, a 31% increase over already record-breaking 2024 levels [5][6].

  • The Physical Bedrock: The boom has moved from chips to “plumbing.” Data center capital expenditures surged 59% year-over-year in late 2025, driven by a massive pivot toward liquid cooling and grid-scale power distribution [7]. For every dollar spent on silicon, hyperscalers are now committing nearly 75 cents to the surrounding infrastructure of power and cooling [8].

The infrastructure build-out is real. The excitement is justified. But even revolutions have price tags, and right now, those tags are stretched to the edge of euphoria.

What the Data and History Tell Us

When you zoom out, manias follow a distinct, historical rhythm. They are not chaotic; they are mathematical.

The cycle always moves through four distinct acts: Discovery, where technology expands imagination; Acceleration, as capital floods the zone; Euphoria, when valuations decouple from earnings; and finally, the Reset, which wipes out the speculators to reward the patient.

AI today sits in the precarious gap between acceleration and euphoria.

The fundamentals are undeniably robust. Demand for compute power and model training has physically outpaced global electricity capacity. The supply chain—from high-end GPUs to industrial power transformers—is operating at maximum utilization. However, while the story remains perfect, the numbers are starting to blink red.

The Warning Lights in the Data:

  • Valuation Extremes: The top 10 AI stocks now trade at 50 times forward earnings, while the median software company trades at 22 times. This spread is now more than two standard deviations above the long-term average (10-year mean).

Key Insights

  1. The +2 sigma Breach: The historical average spread between large-cap tech and the median software company is approximately 12 points. At a 28-point spread in late 2025, the market is currently 2.3 standard deviations (>2 sigma) above the 10-year mean.

  2. The “Efficiency” Ceiling: While Top 10 AI stocks (Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, etc.) are seeing valuations cool slightly from mid-2024 peaks (from 54 times to 50 times), the Median Software multiple is pinned at 22 times. This suggests that while “AI leaders” are being given the benefit of the doubt on growth, the rest of the SaaS world is being valued on current cash flow.

  3. Historical Context: At the peak of the dot-com bubble (March 2000), the top four tech names traded at nearly 70 times 2-year forward earnings. In comparison, today’s Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta) trade at a more “reasonable” 26 times expected two-year-ahead earnings, while the hardware-heavy “Top 10” stay closer to the 50 times mark due to cyclical chip demand.

  • Collapsing Breadth: Market participation has narrowed significantly. Fewer than half of the stocks in the Nasdaq are rising, even as the index hits nominal highs, a classic sign of internal exhaustion.

The Gravity of Yields: Real yields have risen 120 basis points from their 2023 lows. In financial physics, higher real yields act as gravity, eventually pulling down inflated valuation multiples.

This is how every mania matures: the fundamentals remain intact, but the prices simply outrun the profits. It is 1998, not 2000. The technology is real, but the market is assuming perfection in an execution-heavy world.

The “Prove It” Phase: The ROIC Gap

Unlike the 2000 bubble, the “Generals” of this era such as Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet are remarkably profitable. The risk today isn’t bankruptcy; it is the Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) Gap.

In 2025, the market’s curiosity has shifted. It is no longer asking, “How many GPUs did you buy?” but rather, “How much revenue did those GPUs generate?” When Capex grows at 30% while AI-attributed revenue grows at 10%, the valuation multiple must eventually contract to meet reality. We have officially entered the “Prove It” phase of the cycle.

How to Navigate the Mania Without Getting Burned

If the first rule of investing in a mania is don’t fight it, the second is don’t drown in it.

The goal isn’t to time the top—that is a fool’s errand—but to build a portfolio capable of surviving the drawdown when the tide inevitably recedes. To do that, we must move from emotional reaction to structural discipline.

1. Separate the “AI Core” from the “AI Hopes”

Every mania has truth at its center and storytelling at its edges. The art of survival is knowing which is which. In 2025, the market has begun to bifurcate, and your allocation must reflect that reality.

The AI Core: The “Railway Engineers”

The Core consists of entities that own the physical and functional necessity of the era. They sit on hard earnings, massive cash flows, and visible backlogs that stretch into 2027. They are the modern equivalents of the 19th-century steel makers—the ones who own the engine, the fuel, and the rails.

  • Key Players: Nvidia (NVDA) and TSMC (TSM) provide the architecture and the forge. Google (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT) own the full vertical stack—from custom silicon and subsea fiber to the model interfaces. Eaton (ETN) and Vertiv (VRT) provide the power and cooling “plumbing” without which the digital brain cannot function.

  • Exposure: Large-cap equity or VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) / iShares AI Infrastructure ETF (EXI).

The AI Hopes: The “Speculative Frontier”

The Hopes consist of companies living on promise, narrative, and momentum. They represent the “optionality” in your portfolio—the high-beta satellites that may become the giants of 2030, but are currently navigating the perilous “Prove It” phase of 2026.

  • Key Players: Vertical SaaS leaders like Palantir (PLTR) and C3.ai (AI); robotics innovators such as Symbotic (SYM); and speculative hardware like IonQ (IONQ). These firms deserve a seat at the table for exposure, but never for dependence.

  • Exposure: High-growth satellite positions or Global X Robotics & AI ETF (BOTZ) / ARK Autonomous Tech & Robotics ETF (ARKQ).

The Golden Rule of Sizing: If AI is the revolution of our time, the aim isn’t to avoid it but rather to size it. If a 40% sector drawdown would blow up your long-term financial plan, your allocation is too big. A healthy portfolio finances its “hopes” from the realized profits of its “core,” not from new risk.

2. Replace Conviction with Process

P/E ratios are largely useless during revolutions; they measure historical performance in an environment defined by velocity. To survive 2025, you must replace “gut feelings” with emotional automation.

Instead of traditional multiples, use metrics that track how rapidly growth converts into actual liquidity:

  • $EV /Forward Gross Profit

  • $EV /Next-12-Month Free Cash Flow (FCF)

A disciplined process requires pre-set “Guardrails” to override your own greed and fear:

  1. The Exit Trigger: When a stock trades two standard deviations (+2 sigma) above its own 3-year valuation band, you must trim. Not because the company is bad, but because the price has outrun the math.

  2. The Entry Trigger: When a “Core” leader is down 25–30% from its peak, but earnings revisions remain positive, you add.

Structure turns volatility into strategy. By capping “Hopes” at 15%, you maintain upside without risking insolvency.

Discipline beats bravado. You don’t need to know when the music stops; you just need a rule for what to do when it skips a beat.

3. Watch the Five Early Warning Lights.

Manias rarely crash without warning. They start whispering first.
The trick is to listen, not to the headlines, but to the quiet, structural tells in the data.

Across every speculative cycle — from the South Sea Bubble to dot-coms — the market has announced its turning points in roughly the same five ways.
We’re seeing all of them now, to varying degrees.

a) Breadth Narrows (The “Concentration” Trap)

This is the oldest warning sign in the book: when the index rises, but fewer stocks participate in the rally. As of late 2025, the top 10 stocks account for over 40% of the S&P 500’s total market cap—an all-time high. It’s classic late-cycle behavior: the leadership has become dangerously thin.

b) Issuance Spikes (The “Supply” Shock)

Late in every mania, sellers come out to feed the demand. Companies rush to IPO, and retail traders pile into short-dated call options. With single-stock call volumes reaching historic highs of 40 million contracts per day, the market is effectively “selling” its future volatility for present-day hype.

c) Real Yields Rise (The “Oxygen” Drain)

Rising real yields—the inflation-adjusted cost of money—are the oxygen drain that ends speculative surges. At 1.8%, the “cost of capital” has effectively doubled from its lows. For long-duration growth stocks like AI, this is a silent killer of valuation multiples.

d) Earnings Revisions Flatten (The “Expectation” Wall)

A mania is sustainable as long as earnings outpace expectations. But according to the latest data, S&P 500 earnings estimates have essentially stalled. When analysts stop raising forecasts but prices keep soaring, the marginal dollar is being driven by sentiment, not substance.

e) Volatility Vanishes (The “Complacency” Signal)

When the VIX—Wall Street’s “fear gauge”—sits in the low teens, it means investors have stopped pricing in risk. Every major market correction of the last decade began from a similar level of false calm.

The Survival Directive: When three or more of these lights flash Red, history says it’s time to shift from offense to defense. This doesn’t mean you run for the exits; it means you trim your winners, build your T-bill ladder, and stop assuming the next quarter’s return will look like the last.

Manias always sound rational at their peak. But the data never lies—you just have to listen.

4. Energy: The New Silicon

In 2023, the bottleneck was H100 chips. In 2025, the bottleneck is Grid Capacity.

You cannot disrupt physics with code. While software scales at zero marginal cost, the electrical grid requires copper, transformers, and years of permitting. The most resilient “shovels” in this mania are no longer just the chip designers—they are the companies solving the physical constraints of intelligence.

  • The Grid: Vertiv, Eaton, and Schneider Electric. These are the “plumbing” providers of the data center, turning raw high-voltage power into the stabilized current a GPU requires.

  • The Cooling: As power density increases, air cooling is failing. Liquid cooling providers are now the gatekeepers of next-gen server clusters.

  • The Landlords: Data Center REITs (like Equinix or Digital Realty) own the “digital dirt.” In a world of infinite software, specialized physical space is the ultimate scarcity.

The Insight: As margins eventually compress for chipmakers due to competition, profits migrate down the chain to the infrastructure that cannot be easily replicated.

5. Size for Survival

Conviction is a virtue, but concentration is a risk. Here is the uncomfortable math of 2025: If your AI exposure dropped 50%, and your total portfolio fell by more than 10–12%, you are not “invested”—you are overexposed.

6. Hedging as Insurance, Not Speculation

Sophisticated investors do not hedge because they are “bears”; they hedge so they can afford to remain “bulls.” Think of a hedge as a seatbelt: you don’t wear it because you plan to crash; you wear it so you can drive fast with confidence.

  • The 2% Rule: Allocate 1–2% of your portfolio’s annual value to “portfolio insurance”—typically 3–6 month index or sector puts. This is a sunk cost, like a premium, that protects against a “black swan” event.

  • The “Trim & Tuck” Strategy: When a core holding hits a “Euphoria” level (trading at +2 sigma above its 3-year mean valuation), trim 10% of the position. “Tuck” that capital into a 5% yielding T-bill ladder.

This process provides the liquidity needed to buy the dip when the speculative “Hopes” sector inevitably corrects. You aren’t betting against AI; you are betting on your own ability to stay in the game.

7. Benchmark Your Progress

You cannot manage what you do not measure. In the AI era, comparing your performance solely to the S&P 500 is misleading because the index itself is now an AI-heavy proxy.

A sensible benchmark for a modern AI-tilted allocation blends three elements:

  1. Nasdaq-100 (QQQ): For software and large-cap tech.

  2. Semiconductor Index (SOX): For the hardware cycle.

  3. MSCI ACWI: For global equity context.

If your portfolio beats this mix with smaller drawdowns, you are practicing disciplined investing. If you are only winning because you are taking more risk, you haven’t found a strategy—you’ve just found a temporary tailwind.

AI in the Total Portfolio

Avoid the “Concentration Trap” by treating AI as a high-performance engine, not the entire vessel. Use a Core-Satellite Framework to maintain equilibrium:

  • The Global Core (60–70%): Broad, low-cost index exposure (MSCI ACWI / S&P 500). This captures the Productivity Dividend—adoption by non-tech firms—without the “Hype Premium.”

  • The AI Tactical Sleeve (15–20%): Your alpha engine of “Shovels” and profitable “Generals.” This is the only sleeve subject to the +2 sigma Trim Rule.

  • Liquid Ballast (15–20%): Cash, T-bills, and Gold. With real yields at 1.8%, this is Optionality—the dry powder needed to buy the reset when the “music skips.”

In a market “reset,” AI stocks, Bitcoin, and the Nasdaq-100 move in lockstep. Owning five different chipmakers isn’t diversification; it’s concentration in different wrappers. True balance requires assets that “hate” what AI “loves.” If AI thrives on high liquidity, your ballast must thrive on volatility.

When a parabolic run pushes your AI sleeve from 15% to 25%, harvest. Rebalance those gains into your Core or Ballast. This isn’t a lack of conviction—it is the mechanical conversion of speculative momentum into permanent structural wealth.

The Long Game: Surviving the Engine of Progress

We often treat market manias as collective delusions—moments where humanity loses its grip on math. But history suggests a different perspective: Manias are not mistakes; they are the high-octane fuel of progress.

They are the mechanism by which society “over-invests” in the future, funding the infrastructure of a new world at a speed that rational capital would never allow.

  • The Railway Mania of the 1840s left thousands of investors penniless, but it gifted Britain the physical backbone of an industrial empire.

  • The Dot-com Crash of 2000 wiped out trillions in “paper wealth,” but it laid the fiber-optic cables that made the modern, hyper-connected economy possible.

  • The AI Mania is currently doing the same. It is funding a planetary-scale build-out of compute, energy, and connectivity that will serve as the foundation for the next century of intelligence.

The Darwinism of the Drawdown

The technology will survive. The infrastructure will remain. But not every investor will make it to the other side.

The difference between a “speculator” and a “survivor” isn’t their ability to predict the future—it’s their ability to endure it. The market eventually punishes those who confuse a great story with a great price, but it rewards those who use discipline as their anchor.

We are living through one of the most significant capital formation events in human history. To be here is a privilege; to stay here requires a process.

“The future doesn’t belong to the loudest voice in the room or the boldest bet on the board. It belongs to the investor who builds, hedges, and rebalances—the one who understands that in a revolution, staying solvent is the ultimate act of defiance.”

Manias don’t die. They simply mature, quietly compounding in the portfolios of those who were prepared for the music to skip a beat.
