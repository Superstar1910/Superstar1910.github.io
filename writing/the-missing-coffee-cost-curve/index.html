The Missing Coffee Cost Curve

We publish coffee prices more readily than we measure who can produce at those prices.

There is no shortage of coffee price headlines. But a farmer deciding whether to prune, replant, fertilise or sell cannot rely on the headline alone. The more consequential number is often cost per saleable kilogram, which varies with productive trees, yield, harvest labour, farm age and processing outturn.

Imagine two one-acre farms with identical cash expenses and very different harvests. The higher-output farm distributes common costs across more kilograms. Yet it would be equally misleading to attribute the entire difference to management quality: varieties, weather, soil, age of trees and the labour supplied by the household may differ. A credible cost curve must stratify these factors rather than average them away.

The Global Coffee Data Standard provides a useful structure for recording production costs per kilogram of green-bean equivalent. Fairtrade's Living Income Reference Price methodology adds another important perspective: the price required under sustainable yield and livelihood assumptions. This is a normative reference case—not the same thing as an observed farmgate price or the median cost of production.

For Uganda we need farm-year records that document tree survival, planted area, fresh harvested weight, processing route, actual saleable output, cash expenses and unpaid labour. Establishment and replanting costs should be considered over the productive lifecycle rather than casually charged to the current harvest.

The Coffee Pricing Engine's farm module calculates cash, accounting and economic costs separately. Its present values are user-entered and illustrative; no national distribution has yet been calibrated. The ambition is to build a defensible cost curve that can be challenged by growers and researchers.

Contribute evidence

A small number of well-measured farm years is more useful for initial validation than a large set of unlabelled price claims. We welcome anonymised records and sampling-method advice via Legesi.xyz.
