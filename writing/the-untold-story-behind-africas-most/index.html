The Untold Story behind Africa’s Most Valuable Fintech Spinout

Originally published on Substack: 2025-05-07 · Original publication ↗

🧭 TLDR

MTN has created one of Africa’s most valuable fintech assets, valued at $5.2 billion by Mastercard. Yet investors in the listed MTN subsidiary OpCos are largely pricing these OpCos as traditional dividend telcos. Ownership of mobile money (MoMo) operations remains within these listed subsidiaries, but the market may not fully recognise the digital optionality hidden in these stocks.

Mispricing in Frontier Markets: The Opportunity and the Frustration

In mature markets, when companies unlock new high-growth divisions, valuations respond swiftly.

Stripe, for example, despite a downround, recently touched a ~20x revenue multiple due to its digital payments growth. Adyen in Europe, and Block (formerly Square) in the US, have seen reratings as investors priced in platform scalability.

"A rerating happens when the market assigns a higher value to a company’s earnings or revenues—not because profits have suddenly grown, but because perceptions have shifted. Reratings reflect changing investor expectations around growth, risk, or strategic clarity. In frontier markets like Africa, reratings can be powerful, but also delayed, as liquidity, narratives, and structural reforms often lag behind fundamentals. Recognizing when a business deserves a higher multiple—and positioning early—can be one of the most rewarding strategies in investing."

However, in frontier markets, even when real value is created, it may not be priced fairly - or timely. This was alluded to by Mr. Japheth Katto in his comment on my article on Stanbic, regarding what I am seeing as a shareholder. I have repeated the comment below.

“…Question for you: what is your view on the share price of Stanbic at the USE given all the positives in your article? May be this is an article for another day, not only on the Stanbic share price but other listed companies of your choice. Thank you for keeping us enlightened.”

I shall return to Stanbic another day but I thought I would explore this topic through the lens of MTN Group vs the MTN Country Operating Companies (OpCos).

Markets rarely reward fundamentals alone. For a rerating to happen, investors usually need a catalyst:

  • strategic clarity

  • cleaner disclosures

  • stronger capital discipline

  • or a visible monetization event like an IPO

In frontier markets, the gap between fundamentals and valuations can persist until such catalysts make the hidden value undeniable. This is the crux of this article.

To set the scene, and to explain why I’ve been invoking names like Stripe, Adyen, and Block, even though they are not African companies, we first need to understand MTN and how markets may reward or fail to reward frontier market growth stories.

MTN from telecom giant to fintech powerhouse, 1994 - 2024

MTN’s roots stretch back to 1994, when it launched operations in South Africa as a state-licensed mobile operator serving a handful of thousand early adopters. Over the next two decades, MTN expanded aggressively across the continent, building a pan-African voice and data network that now connects over 290 million telecom subscribers across 19 countries.

But around 2014–2015, a deeper shift began. Recognizing the limits of traditional voice and data growth, MTN started laying the groundwork for mobile financial services. Initially basic money transfers, then later expanded into merchant payments, remittances, savings, and microcredit. By 2018, MTN Mobile Money (MoMo) had crossed 30 million users. By 2020, despite economic lockdowns across Africa, MoMo registered transaction volumes surged to over 9 billion transactions worth $152 billion, representing a sharp acceleration.

As of FY2024, MTN’s mobile money ecosystem now serves more than 69 million active MoMo users, with annual transaction values exceeding $260 billion across its markets. Importantly, MoMo revenue has grown from a side business into a significant earnings contributor: accounting for approximately 25–35% of service revenues in markets like Uganda and Ghana. Meanwhile, the number of telco subscribers continues to expand, reaching nearly 290 million across the footprint. However, it is the growth of MoMo that has become the real catalyst for future earnings and valuation upside.

This evolution did not happen by accident. MTN undertook a deliberate restructuring by licensing standalone MoMo entities like MobileMoney Ltd in Ghana (2022) and MTN Mobile Money Uganda Ltd (2021), preparing for regulatory independence and future financial innovation. Operational carve-outs, separate boards, improved disclosures, and technological investments followed. More on this below.

Finally, in 2023, external validation arrived, Mastercard, one of the world’s leading digital payments companies, agreed to invest $200 million for a minority stake in MTN Group Fintech at a $5.2 billion valuation.

Yet despite this extraordinary journey, from a basic mobile operator to a sophisticated digital financial platform, market perceptions have not fully caught up. Investors, continue to price the listed MTN OpCos largely as mature, dividend-yielding telecom stocks. They fail to reflect the fintech optionality, the scale network effects, or the new revenue diversification emerging inside the company.

The $5.2 Billion Question and the Value mismatch

On 14 August 2023, the Financial Times and other media reported that Mastercard was investing $200 million into MTN Group Fintech, acquiring a minority stake.

This investment, values the MTN Fintech Group business at $5.2 billion on a cash and debt free basis and introduces Mastercard as both a strategic investor and operational partner. The transaction with Mastercard brings key enablers in fraud prevention, KYC/fraud management, digital identity, and cross-border and global payment interoperability.

When a company is valued 'on a cash and debt free basis,' it is essentially being valued on its Enterprise Value (EV). Enterprise Value reflects the worth of a company’s core operating assets, independent of how those assets are financed. It excludes cash (which reduces risk) and debt (which adds risk), focusing purely on the business’s ability to generate profits and growth. In the MTN Fintech deal, Mastercard’s $5.2 billion valuation represents what the business would be worth to any buyer, before accounting for financing structure or balance sheet strength.

What is the value of MTN Fintech Business?

Mastercard’s $200 million investment which values MTN Group Fintech at $5.2 billion, implies an enterprise value (EV)/revenue multiple of ~5.2x based on FY2023 MTN fintech revenue of ~$1.0 billion.

The Link between Enterprise Value, Equity Value and therefore share price

Enterprise Value is linked to Equity value through a formula:

Market value of equity + Market value of debt - Cash = Enterprise Value

Applying this to MTN Uganda, given a Market Capitalisation of UGX 6.05 trillion and an enterprise value of approximately UGX 7.28 trillion, implies [Market value of debt - Cash] = UGX 1.23 trillion.

Market Value of Equity = Enterprise Value - [Market Value of Debt - Cash]

Market Value of Equity = UGX 9.15 trillion - UGX 1.23 trillion = UGX 7.92 trillion, into 22.45 billion shares which implies a share price of UGX 352.7, versus the current trading price of UGX 270 as of 30 April 2025.

This suggests an upto 30% upside on the current share price of UGX 270.

But who really owns the fintech (MoMo) value?

Currently, MTN Group’s strategic direction involves transitioning its mobile money operations into a fintech-focused structure under "MTN Group Fintech." The MTN Group Fintech consolidates mobile financial services across Africa, including. An evolution of MTN Fintech Group over the past four (4) years towards this is shown below.

In preparation for the Mastercard deal and in response to regulatory requirements, each country has operationally carved out MoMo as standalone legal entities.

These MoMo entities may eventually be consolidated under MTN Group Fintech Holdings B.V., a private limited company incorporated in the Netherlands in 2013 (Co. No. 57916667). While this holding structure is currently dormant from a public reporting perspective, it reflects MTN’s intent to centralize ownership for strategic investment participation and future monetization.

However, while this transformation is underway, the legal and financial structures remain varied across key markets:

  • Uganda: MTN Mobile Money Uganda Ltd remains a wholly owned subsidiary of MTN Uganda Ltd, providing direct exposure to fintech revenues and earnings.

  • Ghana: MTN Ghana owns MobileMoney Ltd outright, and financial performance is fully consolidated within Scancom PLC.

  • Nigeria: MoMo PSB is a wholly owned subsidiary of MTN Nigeria Communications Plc, following the 2024 acquisition of the remaining 20% stake. The entity is fully consolidated in MTN Nigeria’s results.

While the ultimate vision may be to consolidate MoMo entities under a future MTN Fintech Group holding structure, this is not yet the current legal reality in any of the three core markets.

None of the reviewed annual reports for Uganda, Ghana, or Nigeria provide explicit strategic guidance on whether MoMo earnings will remain within the listed telecom entities or eventually be redirected to a group-level fintech structure. All three listed entities currently retain their MoMo earnings, which are fully consolidated.

Without that clarity, investors may find it difficult to assess the sustainability of current earnings and dividend flows derived from MoMo operations.

As operations scale, and with enhanced disclosures and investor engagement, the market could begin to more accurately value the embedded fintech businesses. However, the timing of this is not clear or certain.

Questions That Remain

As the valuation debate around MTN's fintech business at each OpCo unfolds, some critical questions remain unanswered. It is incumbent upon MTN to provide clear guidance to its shareholders. First, clarity is needed on whether future dividends from the MoMo business will be retained within MTN OpCo for the benefit of local shareholders, or whether they will be upstreamed to MTN Group at the parent level. Second, it remains to be seen whether the MoMo entities will be spun off and listed as separate publicly traded companies, allowing investors to directly participate in the fintech growth story. Third, to enable a more accurate valuation of the business, shareholders require greater transparency through segment-level financial disclosures for the MoMo operations. The resolution of these issues will influence shareholder value realization and determine the degree to which MTN’s fintech opportunity is accessible to public market investors.

What This Means for Shareholders

The outcome of these unresolved questions have implications for shareholders across the MTN ecosystem, particularly as the Group moves ahead with its proposed fintech platform structure.

For MTN Group (JSE) shareholders, there is clear exposure to the consolidated value of the fintech business through Mastercard’s minority investment, the capture of growing fintech revenues, and the potential upside from a future IPO of the Group’s consolidated fintech platform. However, for shareholders in MTN OpCos, the situation is more complex. The absence of detailed segment-level financial disclosures may create a valuation gap. The market cannot fully see or appropriately value the fintech earnings embedded in each OpCo. This misalignment between the Group’s fintech value realization strategy and the limited visibility provided to OpCo shareholders may raise concerns about whether local investors will capture the full benefits of the fintech growth opportunity.

Conclusion

MTN (MoMo) has shown that fintech in Africa is no longer a theoretical story. MTN has built the rails, and Mastercard’s investment validates the reality and value of that infrastructure, shows a path to exit for a fintech investment. The debate is not about whether MoMo has value, it is undeniable. The critical question now is who owns that value and how can it be unlocked?

If you are an investor in MTN, what matters now is making sense of how MTN is restructuring its businesses, what it is (and isn’t) telling the public, and how profits from mobile money will be shared. As the market starts to recognize the true value of MTN’s fintech side, those who have taken the time to understand who really owns what and how it is all set up will have the best shot at benefiting from the upside.
