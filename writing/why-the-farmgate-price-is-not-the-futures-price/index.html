Why the Farmgate Price Is Not the Futures Price

A futures benchmark is a reference contract, not a physical purchase at any farm gate.

A headline futures quote and a farmer's selling price can move in the same market without describing the same asset. Futures refer to standardised contracts, specifications and delivery dates. A physical sale requires species, grade, moisture, place, quantity, shipment window and payment terms. A farmer may be selling cherry or Kiboko while the global quote relates to specified green coffee.

Compare the physical product first

The correct comparison begins by matching kilograms. Drying removes water. Hulling removes hulls. Sorting separates grades and rejects. A domestic input price must therefore be translated through actual measured outturn, not a universal conversion ratio, before comparison with a green export contract. The International Coffee Organization publishes coffee market indicators, but an indicator is not an executable farmgate bid.

Basis, costs and contractual terms

An exporter also bears a location and quality basis: transport, warehousing, export compliance, financing and sometimes marine freight or insurance, depending on the agreed Incoterm. Currency conversion has its own date and spread. The difference between exchange futures and a local price is consequently a collection of contract, quality, place, time and market effects—not a single guaranteed arbitrage.

Colombia's coffee purchasing references provide an institutional example of combining international benchmarks, exchange rates and country differentials. See the Federación Nacional de Cafeteros de Colombia for its domestic purchasing-price reference. Replicating that transparency in East Africa is a worthwhile research direction, but any Ugandan grade differential needs observation and validation rather than inference.

What a responsible pricing engine should display

A sound pricing tool displays the futures benchmark, domestic indicative observation and executable buyer bid as separate, labelled series. It computes a credible commercial netback only where the underlying physical contract is comparable. That requires source and observation date, grade and physical form, measured outturn, currency and FX date, delivery basis, cost schedule and payment timing.

Contribute evidence

We seek dated, anonymised physical quotations with grade, form, place and payment terms, as well as critique of regional price bridges. Start a conversation before sharing confidential records.

Research programme: Coffee Pricing Engine. The modelling research is evolving. No market quotations or quantitative price differentials are asserted in this essay.
