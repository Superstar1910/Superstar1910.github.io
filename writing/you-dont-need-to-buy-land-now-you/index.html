You Don’t Need to Buy Land Now. You Just Need the Option.

Originally published on Substack: 2025-04-19 · Original publication ↗

Africa needs to invest $130–170 billion annually in infrastructure just to meet basic growth and development needs1. But only about half of that is actually financed and delivered each year.

Why?

Yes, financing gaps matter. But there’s a deeper, often-overlooked problem: land access.

  • Housing projects are delayed years due to land titling issues.

  • Road and energy corridors get stuck in compensation disputes.

  • Developers sit on cash but can’t secure land in time or at the right price.

Land is the choke point—and yet we continue to treat it as an afterthought.

This article is for:

  • Project Developers who need clean, ready-to-develop land but can’t risk millions upfront.

  • Governments and Public Agencies trying to implement infrastructure masterplans but battling land bottlenecks.

  • Landowners and Families who want to monetize their land—without being cheated or forced to sell now.

The tool? Land options. Simple in theory. Powerful in practice. Criminally underused.

A land option gives a buyer (developer or public body) the right—but not the obligation—to buy land at a fixed price within a set timeframe. In return, the landowner is paid a non-refundable option premium.

It’s like putting land on layaway: you pay a little now to secure the right to pay the rest later—after you’ve secured planning approval, financing, or project green lights.

The key components of a land option are:

  • Option Premium: Typically 2–8% of the land value2,3

  • Option Period: Usually 6–36 months

  • Strike Price: Pre-agreed land sale price

  • Condition: Buyer exercises only if project proceeds

Case Studies Across Africa

1. Kigali, Rwanda – Urban Expansion Planning

The city government mapped key future zones and optioned strategic land parcels along planned roads. Landowners got a 3% premium and guaranteed market-price purchase later. Planners got certainty. Developers got a green light once infrastructure was in place4.

2. Lekki, Nigeria – Private Land Assembly

Developers aiming to build housing estates optioned plots across 200 acres, spreading risk while negotiating with dozens of families. As land values rose, they locked in 2020 prices in a 2023 market5.

  • Average land price in Lekki: ₦35 million/plot

  • Option premiums: ₦1.5–₦2 million, 3–5% of plot value

  • Net savings per plot after exercising: ₦4–₦6 million

3. Kenya – Urban Development and Energy Projects

In Kenya, developers have applied land options and similar agreements to secure land rights while minimizing upfront costs.

In urban real estate, the Tatu City development—a 5,000-acre Special Economic Zone near Nairobi—used phased acquisition and option-like deals with multiple landowners. Initial premiums were estimated at 3–5% of land value, with full payment deferred until planning and infrastructure approvals were in place6.

In renewable energy, the Kipeto Wind Power Project in Kajiado County engaged local communities through pre-lease agreements, offering KES 20,000–50,000 upfront for land access and committing to annual lease payments once construction began6.

These flexible land access models helped de-risk projects and align community interests—without requiring full land purchase from day one.

Why land options matter

Problem vs Solution solved via land options:

  • Land is expensive to buy upfront: Pay a small premium to secure it for later

  • Delays due to title or planning: Use option period to sort out paperwork

  • Landowners reluctant to sell immediately: Give them cash now + a fixed price guarantee

  • Investors want certainty before buying: Lock in land before committing to full investment

A 5-Step Framework for Using Land Options in Projects

Step 1: Identify Strategic Land Needs

Use Geographic Information System (GIS) tools or masterplans to target land near roads, utilities, or growth corridors7.

Step 2: Engage Landowners

Offer a transparent deal: a small premium now, with a clear strike price later. Build trust.

Step 3: Structure Legal Agreements

Use standardized templates.

Define:

  • Premium %

  • Duration

  • Conditions for exercising

  • What happens if it lapses8

Step 4: Secure Approvals & Financing

Use the option period to finalize planning permission, Environmental and Social (E&S) clearance, and financing.

Step 5: Execute or Exit

If all goes well—exercise. If not—the landowner keeps the premium and can re-market the land.

Use Case: Affordable Housing

A housing developer in Uganda needs 10 acres for 200 homes near a new industrial park.

  • Land price = UGX 1.5 billion

  • Option premium = UGX 90 million (6%)

  • Option term = 12 months

The developer uses the 12-month window to:

  • Apply for government housing subsidies

  • Finalize engineering plans

  • Pre-sell units to buyers

Once ready, they pay the UGX 1.5 billion and start construction—without losing capital if approvals fall through.

What's the Potential?

  • Public Sector: Pre-option land around infrastructure projects to control urban sprawl and speculative land grabs.

  • Private Developers: De-risk early-stage investment in energy, transport, and real estate projects.

  • Landowners: Monetize idle land while maintaining ownership—until a good deal comes along.

Land shouldn’t be what holds Africa’s infrastructure future hostage—it should be what unlocks it. From housing estates in Uganda to energy corridors in Kenya and urban expansion in Nigeria and Rwanda, land options are quietly proving their worth as a flexible, low-risk tool for getting projects off the ground. Whether you are a developer trying to de-risk early-stage investment, a government planning growth corridors, or a landowner looking to earn from idle property—this tool belongs in your playbook. Now is the time to bring it into the mainstream, share success stories, and shape a smarter way forward.
